# Brag Plan: OmniPi

## What is this app?
One independent, read-only place to search mathematical π and inspect public Pi Network records.

## The angle
The same two letters point to two very different worlds. The edit turns a π digit search into a live-ledger view without implying the math and blockchain are related.

## Hook
“One symbol. Two universes.” A large gold π resolves into the two working modes.

## Key moments and user flow
- Enter 14159 in the actual digit-search design; result: first occurrence at digit 1 with highlighted context.
- Switch to Pi Network; recent Mainnet ledgers appear with source and read-only status.
- End on both modes and the GitHub repository.

## Tone and format
- Preset: polished
- Direction: quiet premium product film with quick, precise interaction beats
- Landscape 1920x1080, 21 seconds, no narration.

## Visual identity
- Background: #101b34 / #f7f8fc
- Accent: #d9bd83 and #4568d9
- Text: #17213b
- Display: Manrope or system sans; body: DM Sans or system sans
- Strongest element: gold π and the two lens cards

## Audio direction
Original generated restrained electronic bed and two soft interaction accents. No licensed third-party music. Warm low chord, small pulse at the digit result and ledger reveal, fade out. No speech.

## Storyboard
### 0–3.5s — Two worlds
“One symbol. Two universes.” Gold π resolves on navy. Hold the text for at least 1.5s.
### 3.5–9s — Find the pattern
Recreate the search card. Type 14159, press Search; the result lands: “Digit 1” and a highlighted 14159 in context. Keep results settled for at least 2s.
### 9–14.5s — Follow the ledger
Move to the real Pi Network view, show Mainnet and recent public ledger rows, then a ledger detail. Rows arrive sequentially, with enough time to read the first.
### 14.5–18s — One place
Both mode cards align. “Search the decimal universe. Read the public ledger.”
### 18–21s — Signature
OmniPi logo and “Explore both.” GitHub address below. Hold at least 2s.

## Share copy
OmniPi puts two worlds under one symbol: search 1,000,000 digits of π and explore the Pi Network's public ledger. Open source and read only.
